Defines the complexification of an inner product space $E$ over $\mathbb{K} \in \{\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}\}$ (`RCLike`) as $E \times E$ with the $L^2$ norm, writing $(x, y)$ for $x + i y$. Complex scalars act by $(a+bi)(x,y) = (ax - by,\ bx + ay)$, and the complex inner product is $\langle (x,y),(z,w)\rangle = \operatorname{Re}(\langle x,z\rangle + \langle y,w\rangle) + i\,\operatorname{Re}(\langle x,w\rangle - \langle y,z\rangle)$. So it is the complexification of the underlying *real* space, which makes it a complex Hilbert space. The PR then complexifies continuous linear maps, $T \mapsto T_{\mathbb{C}} = T \times T$, and shows that $T_{\mathbb{C}}$ has the same norm, adjoint, kernel and range, the same real spectrum, and keeps positivity, normality and so on. It also characterises complexified submodules and operators as those invariant under conjugation (two exercises in Roman's *Advanced Linear Algebra*). A second file identifies the complexification with $\mathbb{C} \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} E$ and deduces results on rank, bases, tensor products and matrices.

## Complexification

**Definition.** `Complexification 𝕜 E` is a type synonym for `WithLp 2 (E × E)`, i.e. $E \times E$ with $\|(x,y)\| = \sqrt{\|x\|^2 + \|y\|^2}$. The field $\mathbb{K}$ is a phantom parameter, used only to choose the complex scalar action and inner product later. Anonymous instances give it the `NormedAddCommGroup` and `CompleteSpace` structure of `WithLp 2 (E × E)`.

**Check.** The phantom `𝕜` (with `nolint unusedArguments`) is unusual. A reviewer may prefer to complexify only real spaces and apply it to `RestrictScalars`, or at least want the design choice explained in the docstring.

## instSMul

**Definition.** For an inner product space $E$ over $\mathbb{K}$, the complex scalar action $z \cdot (x,y) = (\operatorname{Re} z\, x - \operatorname{Im} z\, y,\ \operatorname{Im} z\, x + \operatorname{Re} z\, y)$, where real numbers act through $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{K}$. An anonymous instance right after `im_smul` proves the `Module ℂ` axioms using `ext`, `simp`, `module` and `grind`.

The defining formula of the scalar action above.

**Proof.** `rfl`.

## re_smul

$\operatorname{re}(z v) = \operatorname{Re} z \cdot \operatorname{re} v - \operatorname{Im} z \cdot \operatorname{im} v$.

**Proof.** `rfl`.

## im_smul

$\operatorname{im}(z v) = \operatorname{Im} z \cdot \operatorname{re} v + \operatorname{Re} z \cdot \operatorname{im} v$.

**Proof.** `rfl`.

## re_real_smul

**Proof.** Rewrite the real action as the complex one and use `simp`.

## im_real_smul

**Proof.** Same as `re_real_smul`.

**Proof.** `ext; simp`.

**Proof.** Square both sides and expand norms as real parts of $\mathbb{K}$-inner products. The cross terms cancel by symmetry of $\operatorname{Re}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$, then `grind`.

## inclusion

**Definition.** $x \mapsto (x,0)$ as a real linear isometry $E \to E_{\mathbb{C}}$ (for $E$ with a compatible real structure).

## _root_.Submodule.exists_complexification_eq_iff

For a real inner product space $E$ and a complex submodule $U \le E_{\mathbb{C}}$: $U = S_{\mathbb{C}}$ for some real submodule $S \le E$ if and only if $U$ is closed under conjugation. This is Roman, Ch. 1, Ex. 26.

**Proof.** ($\Rightarrow$) is direct. ($\Leftarrow$) Take $S = \{x : (x,0) \in U\}$. Then $(x,y) = (x,0) + i(y,0)$ gives one inclusion. For the other, $\operatorname{re}$ and $\operatorname{im}$ are recovered as $\tfrac12(v + \overline v)$ and $\tfrac1{2i}(v - \overline v)$.

**Check.** Typo "complexificationn" in the docstring.

## toComplexification

**Check.** It is bundled only as an `AddMonoidHom` (it is also $\mathbb{R}$-linear, and an isometry by `isometry_toComplexification`), so later uses may want a `→ₗᵢ[ℝ]` version.

## ofComplexification

**Definition.** Given a complex operator $T : E_{\mathbb{C}} \to_L F_{\mathbb{C}}$ that commutes with $(i_{\mathbb{K}}\cdot)_{\mathbb{C}}$ (the complexification of multiplication by the imaginary unit of $\mathbb{K}$; for $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}$ this is $0$ and the condition is vacuous), the $\mathbb{K}$-linear map $x \mapsto \operatorname{re} T(x,0)$. $\mathbb{R}$-linearity and continuity come from writing it as $\operatorname{re} \circ T \circ \iota$. The hypothesis supplies $\mathbb{K}$-linearity, by splitting $a = \operatorname{Re} a + i\operatorname{Im} a$.

**Proof.** Write $v = (x,0) + i(y,0)$. From $\overline T = T$ and `conj_eq_self_iff`, $T(x,0)$ has zero imaginary part, then compare components.

## exists_toComplexification_eq_iff

**Proof.** ($\Leftarrow$) Take `ofComplexification` and apply the previous lemma. ($\Rightarrow$) Unfold and use `simp`.
