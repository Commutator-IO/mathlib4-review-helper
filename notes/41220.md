Two classical area formulas for a triangle in a real inner product (Euclidean affine) space, stated with mathlib's `Simplex.volume` (the Hausdorff-measure volume) and `Simplex.height` (distance from a vertex to its altitude foot): area $= \tfrac12 \cdot$ height $\cdot$ base, and area $= \tfrac12 ab\sin C$. Along the way it adds that the sine of an angle is unchanged when one ray is replaced by its opposite (collinear points), and expresses the distance to an orthogonal projection as $\sin(\text{angle}) \cdot \text{distance}$. The new file is `Geometry/Euclidean/Volume/Triangle.lean`.

## _root_.Collinear.sin_angle_eq_right

For points $p, q, r, s$ with $q, r, s$ collinear, $q \ne r$ and $r \ne s$:
$$\sin \angle p r q = \sin \angle p r s.$$
(The two angles at $r$ are equal or supplementary.)

**Proof.** Case split on which of the three points lies (weakly) between the others: if $r$ is strictly between $q$ and $s$, the angles sum to $\pi$ and `Real.sin_pi_sub` closes it; in the other two cases $q$ and $s$ lie on the same ray from $r$ and the angles are equal.

## _root_.Collinear.sin_angle_eq_left

Same as the previous lemma with the angles written in the other order: under the same hypotheses, $\sin \angle q r p = \sin \angle s r p$.

**Proof.** `angle_comm` on both sides, then `sin_angle_eq_right`.

## dist_orthogonalProjection_eq_sin_mul_dist

Let $s$ be an affine subspace whose direction admits orthogonal projection, $q \in s$, $p$ any point and $p'$ the orthogonal projection of $p$ onto $s$. Then
$$\operatorname{dist}(p, p') = \sin \angle p q p' \cdot \operatorname{dist}(p, q).$$

**Proof.** The angle at $p'$ in triangle $p p' q$ is $\pi/2$ (`angle_self_orthogonalProjection`), so this is `sin_angle_mul_dist_of_angle_eq_pi_div_two`. (If $q = p'$ the unoriented-angle convention $\angle p q q = \pi/2$ makes it hold too.)

## dist_orthogonalProjection_eq_sin_mul_dist_of_collinear

With $s$, $p$, $p'$ as above, let $q, r \in s$ with $q \ne r$ and $p', q, r$ collinear. Then
$$\operatorname{dist}(p, p') = \sin \angle p q r \cdot \operatorname{dist}(p, q),$$
i.e. the angle may be measured towards any other point $r$ of the line through $q$ and the foot.

**Proof.** If $p' = q$, then $\angle p q r = \pi/2$ and both sides equal $\operatorname{dist}(p,q)$. Otherwise rewrite with the previous lemma and replace $\angle p q p'$ by $\angle p q r$ using `Collinear.sin_angle_eq_right`.

## height_eq_sin_mul_dist

For a triangle $t$ with vertices $P_1, P_2, P_3$ (indices $i_1, i_2, i_3 \in$ `Fin 3` pairwise distinct), the height from $P_{i_1}$ equals
$$h_{i_1} = \sin \angle P_{i_1} P_{i_2} P_{i_3} \cdot |P_{i_1} P_{i_2}|,$$
the sine of the angle at the adjacent vertex $P_{i_2}$ times the side joining them.

**Proof.** Apply `dist_orthogonalProjection_eq_sin_mul_dist_of_collinear` with $s$ the line through the opposite side; collinearity of the altitude foot with $P_{i_2}, P_{i_3}$ comes from `altitudeFoot_mem_affineSpan_image_compl`, and $P_{i_2} \ne P_{i_3}$ from affine independence.

**Check.** `Triangle.lean` gains `public import Mathlib.Geometry.Euclidean.Altitude` for this one lemma; a reviewer may want to confirm this import is acceptable for that file (or that the lemma belongs elsewhere).

## volume_eq_height_mul

For a triangle $t$ and pairwise distinct $i_1, i_2, i_3 \in$ `Fin 3`, its area (2-dimensional volume) is
$$S = \tfrac12 \, h_{i_1} \cdot |P_{i_2} P_{i_3}|.$$

**Proof.** Reindex the triangle by the permutation sending $i_1, i_2, i_3$ to $0, 1, 2$, then `simp` with the general `volume_eq` (volume $= \frac1n \cdot$ height $\cdot$ volume of the opposite face) and the fact that a 1-simplex has volume equal to the distance of its endpoints.

## volume_eq_mul_sin

For a triangle $t$ and pairwise distinct $i_1, i_2, i_3$,
$$S = \tfrac12 \, |P_{i_1} P_{i_2}| \cdot |P_{i_2} P_{i_3}| \cdot \sin \angle P_{i_1} P_{i_2} P_{i_3},$$
i.e. $S = \tfrac12 ab \sin C$ with $C$ the angle at $P_{i_2}$.

**Proof.** Rewrite with `volume_eq_height_mul` and `height_eq_sin_mul_dist`, then `ring`.
