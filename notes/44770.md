Adds the de Moivre–Laplace theorem: for $0 < p < 1$, the standardised binomial law $(\mathrm{Bin}(n,p) - np)/\sqrt{np(1-p)}$ converges in distribution to $\mathcal N(0,1)$, both as a `TendstoInDistribution` statement and as pointwise convergence of CDFs. First it shows that a sum of $n$ independent Bernoulli$(p)$ variables with values in $\{0,1\}$ has law $\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)$. The proof deduces the theorem from the existing central limit theorem (`tendstoInDistribution_inv_sqrt_mul_sum`). The PR also registers the theorem in `docs/1000.yaml`. Here $p$ lives in the unit interval `I`, $\mathrm{Ber}(1,0,p)$ puts mass $p$ on $1$, and $\mathrm{Bin}(\mathbb R,n,p)$ is the pushforward to $\mathbb R$ of the binomial measure on $\mathbb N$.

## infinitePi_bernoulliMeasure_map_sum

For $p \in [0,1]$ and $n \in \mathbb N$: under the infinite product measure $\bigotimes_{i\in\mathbb N}\mathrm{Ber}(1,0,p)$ on $\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}$, the map $x \mapsto \sum_{k<n} x_k$ has law $\mathrm{Bin}(\mathbb R, n, p)$.

**Proof.** Rewrite the real Bernoulli product as the pushforward of the Prop-valued product $\bigotimes \mathrm{Ber}(\text{True},\text{False},p)$ under $\omega \mapsto (\mathbf 1_{\omega_i})_i$. `binomial` is defined as the cardinality of a `setBernoulli` on $\{0,\dots,n-1\}$, which is also such a pushforward. Then compose the maps and identify $\#\{i<n : \omega_i\}$ with $\sum_{i<n}\mathbf 1_{\omega_i}$.

**Check.** Public lemma without a docstring.

## hasLaw_sum_of_iIndepFun_bernoulli

Let $(X_i)_{i\in\mathbb N}$ be real random variables on $(\Omega,P)$ that are mutually independent, each with law $\mathrm{Ber}(1,0,p)$ ($1$ with probability $p$, $0$ otherwise). Then for every $n$, $\sum_{k<n} X_k$ has law $\mathrm{Bin}(\mathbb R, n, p)$.

**Proof.** Independence gives that $(X_i)_i$ has law $\bigotimes_i \mathrm{Ber}(1,0,p)$ (`iIndepFun.hasLaw_infinitePi`). Compose with the previous lemma.

**Check.** The hypothesis asks for an infinite independent sequence even though only $X_0,\dots,X_{n-1}$ matter. A version for a finite family (`Fin n` or a `Finset`) would be more general.

## tendstoInDistribution_binomial

**de Moivre–Laplace.** Let $p \in [0,1]$ with $p \ne 0$ and $p \ne 1$, and let $Y$ be a random variable with law $\mathcal N(0,1)$ on some probability space. Then, as $n \to \infty$, the identity variable on $(\mathbb R, \mathrm{Bin}(\mathbb R,n,p))$ transformed by $x \mapsto (x - np)/\sqrt{np(1-p)}$ converges in distribution to $Y$.

**Proof.** On $\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}$ with the Bernoulli product measure, set $Z_i = (x_i - p)/\sqrt{p(1-p)}$. These are i.i.d. with mean $0$ and second moment $1$, both computed via `integral_bernoulliMeasure`. The CLT gives $n^{-1/2}\sum_{k<n} Z_k \to Y$. By `hasLaw_sum_of_iIndepFun_bernoulli`, the law of $n^{-1/2}\sum_{k<n} Z_k$ is the pushforward of $\mathrm{Bin}(\mathbb R,n,p)$ under the standardisation.

## tendsto_binomial_real_setOf_le

For $p \ne 0, 1$ and every $x \in \mathbb R$: $\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)\big(\{k \in \mathbb N : (k - np)/\sqrt{np(1-p)} \le x\}\big) \to \Phi(x)$ as $n \to \infty$, where $\Phi$ is the CDF of $\mathcal N(0,1)$. Here $\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)$ is the measure on $\mathbb N$.

**Proof.** Apply the previous theorem with $Y = \mathrm{id}$ on $(\mathbb R, \mathcal N(0,1))$, then use the portmanteau theorem for the set $(-\infty, x]$, whose frontier $\{x\}$ is Gaussian-null. Finally rewrite the pushforward measures as preimages on $\mathbb N$.
