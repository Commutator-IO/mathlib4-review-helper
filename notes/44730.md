Proves that a nonnegative real number is rational if and only if its base-$b$ digit sequence (for $b \ge 2$) is eventually periodic. It builds on `Real.digits` ($\text{digits}(x,b)_i = \lfloor x b^{i+1}\rfloor_{\mathbb{N}} \bmod b$, the $(i+1)$-th digit after the point) and `Real.ofDigits` (the value $\sum_i d_i b^{-(i+1)}$ of a digit sequence) in `Analysis/Real/OfDigits`. It also adds an auxiliary identity on natural-number division to `Data/Nat/Init`.

## mul_div_mod_eq_mod_mul_div

For natural numbers $a, b, n$ (division is floor division), $\lfloor ab/n \rfloor \bmod b = \lfloor (a \bmod n)\, b / n \rfloor$.

**Proof.** The cases $n = 0$ and $b = 0$ are handled by `simp`. Otherwise write $a = (a \bmod n) + n\lfloor a/n\rfloor$, so $\lfloor ab/n\rfloor = \lfloor (a \bmod n)b/n\rfloor + b\lfloor a/n\rfloor$. The remainder mod $b$ is the first term, because that term is $< b$.

## digits_fract

For $b \ge 1$ and $x \ge 0$, the base-$b$ digits of the fractional part $\{x\}$ are the same as those of $x$.

**Proof.** Write $x = \{x\} + \lfloor x\rfloor$. Then $\lfloor x b^{i+1}\rfloor = \lfloor \{x\} b^{i+1}\rfloor + \lfloor x\rfloor b^{i+1}$, and the second term is $\equiv 0 \pmod b$.

**Check.** No docstring (also on the other public theorems in this section except the main one).

## val_digits_natCast_div

For natural numbers $r, s, n$ and $b \ge 1$, the $n$-th digit of $r/s$ (as a natural number) is $\lfloor ((r b^n \bmod s)\cdot b)/s \rfloor$. This is the long-division step.

**Proof.** Unfold `digits`, write $\lfloor r b^{n+1}/s\rfloor$ as a natural-number quotient (`Nat.floor_div_eq_div`), then apply `Nat.mul_div_mod_eq_mod_mul_div`.

## exists_periodic_mod_pow

Private: for $s > 0$, the sequence $d \mapsto r b^{N+d} \bmod s$ is periodic with some positive period $c$ from some index $N$ on.

**Proof.** By pigeonhole two indices $i < j$ give the same remainder. Take $N = i$, $c = j - i$.

## digits_ratCast_eventually_periodic

For $b \ge 1$ and a rational $q \ge 0$, the digit sequence of $q$ is eventually periodic: there are $N$ and $c > 0$ with $\text{digits}(q)_{N+d+c} = \text{digits}(q)_{N+d}$ for all $d$.

**Proof.** Write $q = r/s$ with $r = \text{num}(q)$, $s = \text{den}(q)$. By `val_digits_natCast_div`, digit $n$ depends only on $r b^n \bmod s$, which is eventually periodic by the previous lemma.

**Check.** The hypothesis $0 \le q$ is probably removable: for $q < 0$ every digit is $0$ (since $\lfloor\cdot\rfloor_{\mathbb{N}}$ truncates), so the sequence is trivially periodic.

## exists_ratCast_eq_sum_ofDigitsTerm

Private: every finite partial sum $\sum_{i<n} a_i b^{-(i+1)}$ of a digit sequence is a rational number.

## exists_ratCast_eq_ofDigits_of_eventually_periodic

For $b > 1$, if a digit sequence $a : \mathbb{N} \to \{0,\dots,b-1\}$ is periodic with period $c > 0$ from index $N$ on, then $\sum_i a_i b^{-(i+1)}$ is rational.

**Proof.** Split $\text{ofDigits}(a) = (\text{first } N \text{ terms}) + b^{-N}\,\text{ofDigits}(e)$, where $e$ is the shifted sequence (`ofDigits_eq_sum_add_ofDigits`). Since $e$ is purely $c$-periodic, the same splitting gives $\text{ofDigits}(e) = S + b^{-c}\,\text{ofDigits}(e)$, so $\text{ofDigits}(e) = S/(1 - b^{-c})$ with $S$ rational.

## exists_ratCast_eq_iff_digits_eventually_periodic

For $b > 1$ and real $x \ge 0$: $x$ is rational if and only if its base-$b$ digit sequence is eventually periodic (there exist $N$ and $c > 0$ with $\text{digits}(x)_{N+d+c} = \text{digits}(x)_{N+d}$ for all $d$).

**Proof.** ($\Rightarrow$) is `digits_ratCast_eventually_periodic`. ($\Leftarrow$) The digits of $x$ are those of $\{x\}$ (`digits_fract`), and $\{x\} = \text{ofDigits}(\text{digits}\{x\})$ since $\{x\} \in [0,1)$. So $\{x\}$ is rational by the previous theorem, and $x = \{x\} + \lfloor x\rfloor$ is rational.
