Proves that the symmetric Pascal matrix $P_n = \big(\binom{i+j}{i}\big)_{0\le i,j<n}$ has determinant $1$ over any commutative ring. It goes through the factorisation $P_n = L_n L_n^{\mathsf T}$ with $L_n = \big(\binom{i}{j}\big)$ lower unitriangular (Vandermonde's identity in matrix form, following Edelman–Strang), and then uses the existing `det_mul`, `det_transpose` and `det_of_isLowerTriangular` in `Matrix/Block.lean`.

## isLowerTriangular_choose

For every $n$, the $n\times n$ matrix $L_n = \big(\binom{i}{j}\big)_{i,j\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}}$ with entries in $\mathbb N$ is lower triangular: $\binom{i}{j} = 0$ whenever $i < j$.

**Proof.** Directly from `Nat.choose_eq_zero_of_lt`.

## choose_mul_transpose_choose

Vandermonde's identity in matrix form: over $\mathbb N$, $L_n L_n^{\mathsf T} = P_n$, i.e. for all $0 \le i,j < n$,
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \binom{i}{k}\binom{j}{k} = \binom{i+j}{i}.$$

**Proof.** Turns the sum over `Fin n` into a sum over `range n`, shrinks it to `range (i+1)` (the terms with $k > i$ vanish), rewrites $\binom{i+j}{i}$ with Vandermonde (`Nat.add_choose_eq`) as a sum over the antidiagonal, reindexes that as a sum over `range (i+1)`, and finishes with the symmetry $\binom{m}{m-k} = \binom{m}{k}$ (`Nat.choose_symm`).

## det_pascal

For a commutative ring $R$ and every $n$, the matrix $\big(\binom{i+j}{i}\big)_{0\le i,j<n}$, with entries cast into $R$, has determinant $1$.

**Proof.** Writes the matrix as the image of $P_n$ under $\mathbb N \to R$, rewrites $P_n = L_n L_n^{\mathsf T}$, so the determinant is $\det(L_n)\det(L_n^{\mathsf T}) = \det(L_n)^2$, and $\det L_n = \prod_i \binom{i}{i} = 1$ since $L_n$ is lower triangular.
