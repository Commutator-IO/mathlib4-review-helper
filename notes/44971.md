Adds a lemma about subgroups of a $\mathbb Q$-vector space: if $x$ is indivisible in an additive subgroup $P$, then the only rational multiples of $x$ that lie in $P$ are the integer multiples. It is added at the end of `LinearAlgebra/Unimodular.lean` (the file on unimodular / primitive vectors), in namespace `AddSubgroup`.

## rat_smul_mem_iff

Let $V$ be a $\mathbb Q$-vector space, $P \le V$ an additive subgroup and $x \in P$ indivisible in $P$, meaning: whenever $x = m y$ with $y \in P$ and $m \in \mathbb Z$, then $m = \pm 1$. Then for every $q \in \mathbb Q$,
$$q x \in P \iff q \in \mathbb Z.$$

**Proof.** ($\Leftarrow$) $P$ is closed under integer multiples. ($\Rightarrow$) Write $q = a/b$ in lowest terms and pick Bézout coefficients $u a + v b = 1$. Then $y = u(qx) + v x \in P$ and $b y = (ua + vb)x = x$, so $b$ is a unit, i.e. $b = 1$ and $q = a \in \mathbb Z$ (the scalar identity is checked with `match_scalars` and `linarith`).

**Check.** The lemma has nothing to do with unimodularity in the sense of `Module.IsUnimodular`, and the indivisibility hypothesis is written out inline rather than as an existing notion, so a reviewer may want to discuss whether `Unimodular.lean` is the right file and whether an existing predicate could replace the ad hoc hypothesis.
