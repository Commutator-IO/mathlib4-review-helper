Proves the Prékopa–Leindler inequality for a Haar measure on a finite-dimensional real vector space: if $0 \le a \le 1$ and $f(x)^{1-a}g(y)^a \le j((1-a)x + ay)$ for all $x, y$, then $(\int f)^{1-a}(\int g)^a \le \int j$; and derives the Brunn–Minkowski theorem in its multiplicative form $\mu(A)^{1-a}\mu(B)^a \le \mu((1-a)A + aB)$ and its classical form $\mu(A)^{1/n} + \mu(B)^{1/n} \le \mu(A+B)^{1/n}$ ($n = \dim E$). The proof goes through the one-dimensional case using 1-d Brunn–Minkowski (#44802) and the $[0,\infty]$-valued layer-cake formula (#44813), then induction on dimension via Fubini, then a linear change of variables. New files `Integral/Lebesgue/PrekopaLeindler.lean` and `Measure/Haar/BrunnMinkowski.lean`.

## lintegral_eq_lintegral_Ioc_measure_ofReal_le

Layer cake with bounded range: for a.e.-measurable $f : \alpha \to [0,\infty]$ with $f \le a$ everywhere ($a \in \mathbb{R}$):
$$\int^- f\,d\mu = \int^-_{(0,a]} \mu\{x \mid t \le f(x)\}\,dt.$$

**Proof.** If $a < 0$ then $f = 0$ and both sides vanish; otherwise apply `lintegral_eq_lintegral_measure_ofReal_le` and note the integrand is $0$ for $t > a$.

## lintegral_eq_lintegral_Ioo_measure_ofReal_le

**Proof.** $(0,a)$ and $(0,a]$ are a.e. equal for Lebesgue measure.

## prekopa_leindler_real_of_iSup_eq_one

(Private.) One-dimensional Prékopa–Leindler for measurable $f, g, j : \mathbb{R} \to [0,\infty]$ with $\sup f = \sup g = 1$: if $f(x)^{1-a}g(y)^a \le j((1-a)x+ay)$ for all $x,y$, then $(\int f)^{1-a}(\int g)^a \le \int j$.

**Proof.** Weighted AM–GM bounds the left side by $(1-a)\int f + a\int g$. By layer cake on $(0,1)$, this is $\int_0^1 [\lambda((1-a)\{f\ge t\}) + \lambda(a\{g \ge t\})]\,dt$; the level sets are nonempty because $\sup = 1 > t$, so 1-d Brunn–Minkowski bounds the integrand by $\lambda((1-a)\{f\ge t\} + a\{g\ge t\}) \le \lambda\{j \ge t\}$ (by the hypothesis), and layer cake for $j$ finishes.

## prekopa_leindler_real_of_iSup_ne_top

(Private.) Same conclusion when $\sup f$ and $\sup g$ are finite.

**Proof.** If $\sup f = 0$ or $\sup g = 0$, handle directly (cases $a<1$ / $a=1$, resp. $a>0$ / $a=0$). Otherwise rescale $f, g$ by $1/\sup f$, $1/\sup g$ and $j$ by $(\sup f)^{-(1-a)}(\sup g)^{-a}$, apply the previous lemma, and cancel the finite nonzero constant.

## _root_.Real.lintegral_rpow_mul_lintegral_rpow_le

One-dimensional Prékopa–Leindler: for $0 \le a \le 1$ and measurable $f, g, j : \mathbb{R} \to [0,\infty]$ with $f(x)^{1-a}g(y)^a \le j((1-a)x+ay)$ for all $x, y$:
$$\Big(\int f\Big)^{1-a}\Big(\int g\Big)^{a} \le \int j$$
(Lebesgue measure).

**Proof.** Truncate: $f = \sup_n \min(f,n)$, same for $g$; monotone convergence and continuity of $x \mapsto x^{p}$ turn the left side into $\sup_{s,t}(\int\min(f,t))^{1-a}(\int \min(g,s))^a$, and each term is bounded by the bounded case (the truncations still satisfy the hypothesis).

## lintegral_rpow_mul_lintegral_rpow_le_pi

(Private.) Prékopa–Leindler on $\mathbb{R}^n = (\mathrm{Fin}\ n \to \mathbb{R})$ with the product Lebesgue measure, for measurable $f, g, j$ and the hypothesis $f(x)^{1-a}g(y)^a \le j((1-a)x+ay)$.

**Proof.** Induction on $n$; $n=0$ is the hypothesis at the single point. For $n+1$, split $\mathbb{R}^{n+1} = \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^n$ (`measurePreserving_piFinSuccAbove`, Tonelli) and apply the 1-d inequality to the partial integrals $x \mapsto \int f(x, \cdot)$ etc., whose hypothesis is supplied by the induction hypothesis on the fibres.

## lintegral_rpow_mul_lintegral_rpow_le

**Prékopa–Leindler inequality.** Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional real normed space with Borel σ-algebra and $\mu$ an additive Haar measure on $E$. For $0 \le a \le 1$ and measurable $f, g, j : E \to [0,\infty]$ with $f(x)^{1-a}g(y)^a \le j((1-a)x + ay)$ for all $x, y \in E$:
$$\Big(\int f\,d\mu\Big)^{1-a}\Big(\int g\,d\mu\Big)^{a} \le \int j\,d\mu.$$

**Proof.** Take a linear isomorphism $e : \mathbb{R}^n \to E$; $\mu$ pulled back is $c$ times Lebesgue measure. The inequality for $f\circ e, g\circ e, j\circ e$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ multiplied by $c = c^{1-a}c^a$ gives the result.

**Check.** The module docstring states the hypothesis as $f(x)^{1-a}g(x)^a \le j((1-a)x+ay)$; it should be $g(y)$. (Also typo "potentionally" in a comment.)

## measure_rpow_mul_measure_rpow_le

**Brunn–Minkowski, multiplicative form.** For $E$, $\mu$ as above, $0 \le a \le 1$ and nonempty measurable $A, B \subseteq E$:
$$\mu(A)^{1-a}\,\mu(B)^a \le \mu\big((1-a)A + aB\big).$$
The right-hand set need not be measurable.

**Proof.** Take a measurable $C \supseteq (1-a)A + aB$ of the same measure and apply Prékopa–Leindler to the indicators $1_A, 1_B, 1_C$; the pointwise hypothesis is checked by cases, using nonemptiness when $a \in \{0,1\}$.

## measure_rpow_mul_measure_rpow_le₀

Same as the previous theorem with $A, B$ only null-measurable.

**Proof.** Replace $A, B$ by a.e.-equal measurable subsets $A', B'$, add one point of $A$ (resp. $B$) to keep them nonempty, apply the measurable version and monotonicity.

## measure_rpow_add_measure_rpow_le

**Brunn–Minkowski theorem.** For $E$ nontrivial of dimension $n$, $\mu$ a Haar measure, and nonempty measurable $A, B$:
$$\mu(A)^{1/n} + \mu(B)^{1/n} \le \mu(A+B)^{1/n}.$$

**Proof.** The cases $\mu(A)$ or $\mu(B)$ equal to $0$ or $\infty$ are handled by $A + \{b\} \subseteq A+B$. Otherwise, with $\alpha = \mu(A)^{1/n}$, $\beta = \mu(B)^{1/n}$ and $c = \beta/(\alpha+\beta)$, apply the multiplicative form to $(1-c)^{-1}A$ and $c^{-1}B$ with weight $c$, using $\mu(rA) = |r|^n\mu(A)$.

## measure_rpow_add_measure_rpow_le₀

Same as the previous theorem for null-measurable $A, B$.

**Proof.** Same reduction to measurable subsets plus a point as in `measure_rpow_mul_measure_rpow_le₀`.
