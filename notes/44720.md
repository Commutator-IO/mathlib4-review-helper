New file computing the mean $(1-p)/p$ and variance $(1-p)/p^2$ of `geometricMeasure p` (law on $\mathbb N$ of the number of failures before the first success, with mass $(1-p)^n p$ at $n$), for $p \in [0,1]$. For $p = 0$ the measure is defined as $\delta_0$, and since division by zero gives $0$ in Lean both formulas still hold. The proofs use `integral_geometricMeasure` and the series identities `hasSum_coe_mul_geometric_of_norm_lt_one` and `hasSum_sq_mul_geometric_of_norm_lt_one`.

## norm_one_sub_lt_one

Private: for $p \ne 0$ in $[0,1]$, $|1 - p| < 1$.

## hasSum_coe_mul_geometricMeasure

For $p \in (0,1]$, $\sum_{n\ge0} (1-p)^n p \cdot n = (1-p)/p$ (as a `HasSum` in $\mathbb R$).

**Proof.** Multiply $\sum n r^n = r/(1-r)^2$ (with $r = 1-p$) by $p$ and simplify.

**Check.** The statement contains no measure, so the name `..._geometricMeasure` is a bit misleading. It is also a public lemma without a docstring (same for the next one).

## hasSum_sq_mul_geometricMeasure

For $p \in (0,1]$, $\sum_{n\ge0} (1-p)^n p \cdot n^2 = (1-p)(2-p)/p^2$.

**Proof.** Multiply $\sum n^2 r^n = r(1+r)/(1-r)^3$ (with $r = 1-p$) by $p$ and simplify.

## integral_id_geometricMeasure

For every $p \in [0,1]$, $\int n\,d\,\mathrm{Geom}(p)(n) = (1-p)/p$ (both sides $0$ when $p=0$).

**Proof.** Case $p = 0$: $\delta_0$, by `simp`. Otherwise rewrite the integral as the weighted series (`integral_geometricMeasure`) and use `hasSum_coe_mul_geometricMeasure`.

## integral_sq_geometricMeasure

For every $p \in [0,1]$, $\int n^2\,d\,\mathrm{Geom}(p)(n) = (1-p)(2-p)/p^2$.

**Proof.** Same as above, with the second-moment series.

**Check.** No docstring.

## memLp_two_id_geometricMeasure

For every $p \in [0,1]$, the function $n \mapsto n$ is in $L^2(\mathrm{Geom}(p))$.

**Proof.** Case $p = 0$: bounded a.e. under $\delta_0$. Otherwise square-integrability is equivalent to summability of $\sum (1-p)^n p\, n^2$ (`integrable_geometricMeasure_iff`), which follows from the previous series.

## variance_id_geometricMeasure

For every $p \in [0,1]$, the variance of $n \mapsto n$ under $\mathrm{Geom}(p)$ is $(1-p)/p^2$.

**Proof.** $\operatorname{Var} = E[N^2] - E[N]^2 = (1-p)(2-p)/p^2 - (1-p)^2/p^2$, using `variance_eq_sub` and the previous lemmas. Then simplify (the case $p = 0$ separately).
