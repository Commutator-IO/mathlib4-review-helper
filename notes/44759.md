Adds the Binet–Cauchy identity and Lagrange's identity for finite sums in a commutative ring, both written with the double sum over all ordered pairs $(i,j)$ (hence the factor $2$ compared to the usual sum over $i<j$). It then derives Lagrange's identity and the resulting inequality for $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$ (`RCLike`), with absolute values and a complex conjugate.

## sum_sum_mul_sub_mul_mul_mul_sub_mul

Binet–Cauchy identity: for a commutative ring $R$, a finite set $s$ and $a, b, c, d : \iota \to R$,
$$\sum_{i\in s}\sum_{j\in s}(a_ib_j - a_jb_i)(c_id_j - c_jd_i) = 2\Big(\big(\textstyle\sum_i a_ic_i\big)\big(\sum_i b_id_i\big) - \big(\sum_i a_id_i\big)\big(\sum_i b_ic_i\big)\Big).$$

**Proof.** Expands each summand by `ring` into four products, splits the double sum, swaps the order of summation where needed (`sum_comm`), recognises products of single sums (`sum_mul_sum`) and closes with `ring`.

**Check.** These are pure commutative-ring identities with no order hypothesis, yet they are added to `Algebra/Order/BigOperators/Ring/Finset.lean` (outside any section, among ordered-semiring results); an unordered `BigOperators` file may be a better home.

## sum_sum_mul_sub_mul_sq

Lagrange's identity: for a commutative ring $R$, a finite set $s$ and $f, g : \iota \to R$,
$$\sum_{i\in s}\sum_{j\in s}(f_ig_j - f_jg_i)^2 = 2\Big(\big(\textstyle\sum_i f_i^2\big)\big(\sum_i g_i^2\big) - \big(\sum_i f_ig_i\big)^2\Big).$$

**Proof.** The case $a = c = f$, $b = d = g$ of the Binet–Cauchy identity.

## sum_sum_norm_mul_sub_mul_sq

Lagrange's identity over $K = \mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$: for a finite set $s$ and $a, b : \iota \to K$,
$$\sum_{i\in s}\sum_{j\in s}|a_ib_j - a_jb_i|^2 = 2\Big(\big(\textstyle\sum_i |a_i|^2\big)\big(\sum_i |b_i|^2\big) - \big|\sum_i \overline{a_i}\,b_i\big|^2\Big).$$

**Proof.** Casts both (real) sides into $K$, writes each $|z|^2$ as $z\bar z$, and applies the Binet–Cauchy identity with $c = \bar a$, $d = \bar b$, then `ring`.

## sum_sum_norm_mul_sub_mul_sq_le

Over $K = \mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$: $\displaystyle\sum_{i\in s}\sum_{j\in s}|a_ib_j - a_jb_i|^2 \le 2\big(\textstyle\sum_i |a_i|^2\big)\big(\sum_i |b_i|^2\big)$.

**Proof.** Rewrites with the previous identity and drops the nonnegative term $2\,|\sum_i \overline{a_i} b_i|^2$ (`linarith` with `sq_nonneg`).
