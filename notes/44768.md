Proves the "adjacent sequences" theorem in a conditionally complete linearly ordered commutative group with the order topology: if $u$ is monotone, $v$ antitone and $v/u \to 1$ (additively $v - u \to 0$), then $u$ and $v$ converge to a common limit. Sequences are indexed by any nonempty directed preorder $\iota$ (limits along `atTop`). Both lemmas are `@[to_additive]`, producing `Monotone.le_of_antitone_of_tendsto_sub` and `Monotone.exists_tendsto_of_antitone_of_tendsto_sub`; relies on monotone convergence `tendsto_atTop_ciSup`.

## Monotone.le_of_antitone_of_tendsto_div

(Additive version `Monotone.le_of_antitone_of_tendsto_sub`.) If $u$ is monotone, $v$ is antitone and $v_j / u_j \to 1$ along `atTop`, then $u_i \le v_i$ for every $i$ (additively: $v - u \to 0$ implies $u_i \le v_i$).

**Proof.** For $j \ge i$, $v_j/u_j \le v_i/u_i$; passing to the limit gives $1 \le v_i/u_i$ (`le_of_tendsto`).

**Check.** The section variables impose `ConditionallyCompleteLinearOrder H` and `OrderTopology H`, which this lemma does not need (an ordered group with `OrderClosedTopology` suffices); no docstring.

## Monotone.exists_tendsto_of_antitone_of_tendsto_div

(Additive version `Monotone.exists_tendsto_of_antitone_of_tendsto_sub`.) If $u$ is monotone, $v$ is antitone and $v/u \to 1$, then there is $\ell$ with $u \to \ell$ and $v \to \ell$ (additively with $v - u \to 0$).

**Proof.** By the previous lemma $u \le v$ pointwise, so $u$ is bounded above by $v_{i_0}$ (using directedness to compare indices); by monotone convergence $u \to \sup_i u_i =: \ell$, and $v = (v/u)\cdot u \to 1 \cdot \ell = \ell$.
