Let $N$ be a free module over a commutative semiring $R$ with basis $\mathcal C = (c_i)_{i\in\kappa}$, so that every element of $M\otimes_R N$ is uniquely $\sum_i m_i\otimes c_i$ (the existing `TensorProduct.equivFinsuppOfBasisRight`, $M\otimes N \cong \kappa \to_0 M$). The PR shows that $f\otimes \mathrm{id}_N$ acts coordinatewise in this description, and deduces that an element of $P\otimes N$ is in the range of $f\otimes\mathrm{id}_N$ iff all its coordinates are in the range of $f$.

## TensorProduct.equivFinsuppOfBasisRight_rTensor

For an $R$-linear map $f : M \to P$ and $x \in M\otimes_R N$: if $x = \sum_i m_i\otimes c_i$, then $(f\otimes\mathrm{id}_N)(x) = \sum_i f(m_i)\otimes c_i$; i.e. the coordinates of $(f\otimes\mathrm{id}_N)(x)$ in the basis $\mathcal C$ are obtained by applying $f$ to the coordinates of $x$.

**Proof.** Induction on $x$ as a tensor: on pure tensors by `ext; simp`, and additivity of both sides (`Finsupp.mapRange_add`).

## TensorProduct.mem_range_rTensor_iff_equivFinsuppOfBasisRight

For an $R$-linear map $f : M \to P$ and $x \in P\otimes_R N$: $x$ lies in the range of $f\otimes\mathrm{id}_N : M\otimes N \to P\otimes N$ if and only if, writing $x = \sum_i p_i\otimes c_i$, every coordinate $p_i$ lies in the range of $f$.

**Proof.** ($\Rightarrow$) By the previous lemma, the coordinates of $(f\otimes\mathrm{id})(y)$ are $f$ of those of $y$. ($\Leftarrow$) Choose $g_i$ with $f(g_i) = p_i$; then $x = \sum_i p_i\otimes c_i$ is the image of $\sum_i g_i\otimes c_i$ (the sum of elements of the range submodule).
