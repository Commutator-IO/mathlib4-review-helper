Adds the classical congruence $\binom{p^a-1}{k} \equiv (-1)^k \pmod p$ for a prime $p$ and $0 \le k \le p^a-1$, and its corollary that $p \nmid \binom{p^a-1}{k}$. It sits in the Lucas's theorem file next to the other congruences for binomial coefficients modulo a prime, and builds on `Nat.Prime.dvd_choose_pow` ($p \mid \binom{p^a}{k}$ for $0<k<p^a$).

## choose_pow_sub_one_modEq_neg_one_pow

For a prime $p$ and natural numbers $a, k$ with $k \le p^a - 1$ (truncated subtraction, so for $a = 0$ only $k = 0$ is allowed), $\binom{p^a-1}{k} \equiv (-1)^k \pmod p$, as a congruence of integers.

**Proof.** Induction on $k$. For the step, Pascal's rule gives $\binom{p^a-1}{k} + \binom{p^a-1}{k+1} = \binom{p^a}{k+1}$, which is divisible by $p$ since $0 < k+1 < p^a$ (`dvd_choose_pow`). So $\binom{p^a-1}{k+1} \equiv -\binom{p^a-1}{k} \equiv (-1)^{k+1}$.

## not_dvd_choose_pow_sub_one

For a prime $p$ and $k \le p^a - 1$, $p$ does not divide $\binom{p^a-1}{k}$.

**Proof.** If $p$ divided it, the previous congruence would give $(-1)^k \equiv 0 \pmod p$, so $p \mid 1$, contradicting primality.
