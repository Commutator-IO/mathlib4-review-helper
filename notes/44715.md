Proves that for a finitely presented module $M$ over a commutative ring $R$, tensoring with $M$ commutes with arbitrary products: the canonical map $M\otimes_R\prod_i P_i \to \prod_i (M\otimes_R P_i)$ is bijective (Stacks 059K, (1) ⇒ (2); the converse, Lenzing's theorem, is only cited). Along the way it adds that products of exact pairs of maps are exact (`Function.MulExact.piMap`/`prodMap` and their additive versions `Function.Exact.piMap`/`prodMap`), and the case of a finite free module.

## piMap

For families of maps $f_i : \alpha_i \to \beta_i$, $g_i : \beta_i \to \gamma_i$ (each $\gamma_i$ pointed by $1$): if every pair $(f_i, g_i)$ is exact (range of $f_i$ = fibre of $g_i$ over $1$), then $(\prod_i f_i, \prod_i g_i)$ is exact. The additive version `Function.Exact.piMap` (fibre over $0$) is generated by `to_additive`.

**Proof.** Componentwise: if $\prod g_i(y) = 1$, choose preimages $x_i$ of each $y_i$ (axiom of choice) and assemble them; conversely the image of $\prod f_i$ is killed componentwise.

## prodMap

If $(f_1, g_1)$ and $(f_2, g_2)$ are exact pairs, then $(f_1\times f_2,\ g_1\times g_2)$ is exact. Additive version `Function.Exact.prodMap` via `to_additive`.

**Proof.** `simp` with `Set.range_prodMap` and the two exactness hypotheses.

**Check.** `prodMap` is not used anywhere in this PR (only `piMap` is); its inclusion here is a scope question.

## Module.piRightHom_bijective_of_finite_free

For a commutative ring $R$, a finite type $\kappa$ and any family of $R$-modules $(P_i)$, the canonical map $R^\kappa\otimes_R \prod_i P_i \to \prod_i (R^\kappa\otimes_R P_i)$ is bijective.

**Proof.** After composing on both sides with the isomorphisms $R^\kappa \otimes Q \cong Q\otimes R^\kappa \cong Q^\kappa$, the map becomes the swap of indices $(\prod_i P_i)^\kappa \cong \prod_i P_i^\kappa$ (`Equiv.piComm`); the commuting square is checked by induction on tensors.

## Module.FinitePresentation.piRightHom_bijective

For a commutative ring $R$, a finitely presented $R$-module $M$ and any family of $R$-modules $(P_i)_{i\in\iota}$, the canonical map $M\otimes_R\prod_i P_i \to \prod_i (M\otimes_R P_i)$, $m\otimes(p_i)_i \mapsto (m\otimes p_i)_i$, is bijective.

**Proof.** Take a presentation $R^m \xrightarrow{f} R^n \xrightarrow{g} M \to 0$. Tensoring with $\prod_i P_i$, resp. with each $P_i$ and taking the product, gives two right exact rows (right exactness of $\otimes$ plus `Function.Exact.piMap`) connected by the canonical maps; the vertical maps for $R^m$ (surjective) and $R^n$ (bijective) are handled by the finite free case, so the five lemma (`bijective_of_surjective_of_bijective_of_right_exact`) gives bijectivity on $M$.
