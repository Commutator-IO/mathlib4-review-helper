Proves **Euler's theorem in geometry** for a triangle: $|OI|^2 = R(R - 2r)$ for incenter $I$, circumcenter $O$, circumradius $R$ and inradius $r$, and its excenter version $|OI_i|^2 = R(R + 2r_i)$; hence Euler's inequality $2r \le R$, and $r \le$ the nine-point radius $R/2$. It also adds the area formula $S = abc/(4R)$ and a general squared-distance formula from an affine combination to a point. The route: write the in/excenter as an affine combination with weights proportional to the signed side lengths (`excenterWeightsFace` from #44060), expand the squared distance, and use $abc = 4SR$ and $S = r_\pm \cdot |\sum \pm a_i| / 2$. Registered in `docs/1000.yaml`.

## dist_affineCombination_const_sq

For a finite family of points $p_i$ ($i \in s$) in a Euclidean affine space, weights $w_i$ with $\sum_{i\in s} w_i = 1$, and any point $q$, with $X = \sum_i w_i p_i$ the affine combination:
$$|Xq|^2 = \sum_{i \in s} w_i\, |p_i q|^2 - \frac12 \sum_{i,j \in s} w_i w_j\, |p_i p_j|^2.$$

**Proof.** A `calc`: write $X - q = \sum_i w_i (p_i - q)$, expand the squared norm as a double sum of inner products, replace each $\langle p_i - q, p_j - q\rangle$ by the polarisation $\tfrac12(|p_iq|^2 + |p_jq|^2 - |p_ip_j|^2)$, and collapse the first two double sums using $\sum w = 1$.

## sum_excenterWeightsFace_singleton_pos

For an $n$-simplex with $n \ge 2$ and any vertex index $i$, the sum of the signed face volumes with signs $\{i\}$ is positive: $\sum_j w_j > 0$ where $w_i = -\operatorname{vol}(F_i)$ and $w_j = \operatorname{vol}(F_j)$ for $j \ne i$.

**Proof.** Factor $w_j = n \operatorname{vol}(s)\, u_j$ out of the sum and use the existing `sum_excenterWeightsUnnorm_singleton_pos` with `positivity`.

## volume_eq_mul_div_circumradius

For a triangle $t$ and pairwise distinct $i_1, i_2, i_3$, with $a = |P_{i_1}P_{i_2}|$, $b = |P_{i_1}P_{i_3}|$, $c = |P_{i_2}P_{i_3}|$ and circumradius $R$:
$$\operatorname{area}(t) = \frac{abc}{4R}.$$

**Proof.** $S = \tfrac12 a c \sin \angle P_{i_1}P_{i_2}P_{i_3}$ (`volume_eq_mul_sin`) and the law of sines $b / \sin(\text{angle at } P_{i_2}) = 2R$ (`dist_div_sin_angle_div_two_eq_circumradius`), then `field` using $b \ne 0$.

## four_mul_volume_mul_circumradius

Same setting, division-free: $4 \cdot \operatorname{area}(t) \cdot R = abc$.

**Proof.** From the previous lemma, since $R > 0$.

## sum_excenterWeightsFace_pos

For a triangle and signs $\emptyset$, $\{0\}$, $\{1\}$ or $\{2\}$ (the incenter and the three excenters), the sum of the signed side lengths $\sum_i w_i$ is positive. (For a triangle, $w_i = \pm$ the length of the side opposite vertex $i$.)

**Proof.** For $\emptyset$, each side has positive length; for singletons, `sum_excenterWeightsFace_singleton_pos`.

**Check.** The file introduces `local notation "w" => excenterWeightsFace`; a single-letter local notation may be surprising to readers.

## dist_excenter_sq

For a triangle $t$, signs $S \in \{\emptyset, \{0\}, \{1\}, \{2\}\}$, the corresponding in/excenter $E_S$ with weights $\lambda_i$ (`excenterWeights`) and radius $r_S$, and any point $p$:
$$|E_S\, p|^2 = \sum_i \lambda_i |P_i p|^2 + \varepsilon \cdot 2 R\, r_S, \qquad \varepsilon = \begin{cases} -1 & S = \emptyset \\ +1 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$

**Proof.** Apply `dist_affineCombination_const_sq`; write $\lambda_i = w_i/\sum_j w_j$; by explicit case analysis on the four sign sets, $\sum_{i,j} w_iw_j|P_iP_j|^2 / 2 = -\varepsilon\, abc \sum_j w_j$; then $abc = 4SR$ and $S = \tfrac12 r_S \sum_j w_j$ (`volume_eq_exradius_mul`, sum positive) give $\varepsilon \cdot 2Rr_S$.

**Check.** No docstring on this key intermediate result. The proof's middle step is a case split over four sign sets with a large `simp` call followed by `ring` under `all_goals`; possibly fragile.

## dist_excenter_circumcenter_sq

Same sign sets: with $O$ the circumcenter,
$$|E_S O|^2 = R\,(R + 2\varepsilon\, r_S)$$
($\varepsilon = -1$ for the incenter, $+1$ for an excenter).

**Proof.** `dist_excenter_sq` at $p = O$, all $|P_iO| = R$, and $\sum \lambda_i = 1$.

## dist_excenter_singleton_circumcenter_sq

**Euler's theorem for excenters.** For a triangle and any vertex index $i$, the excenter $I_i$ (signs $\{i\}$, the excircle opposite vertex $i$) and exradius $r_i$ satisfy $|I_i O|^2 = R(R + 2r_i)$.

**Proof.** Special case of the previous theorem.

## dist_incenter_circumcenter_sq

**Euler's theorem.** For a triangle, $|IO|^2 = R(R - 2r)$, with $I$ the incenter and $r$ the inradius.

**Proof.** The incenter is the excenter for $\emptyset$; specialise `dist_excenter_circumcenter_sq`.

## two_mul_inradius_le_circumradius

**Euler's inequality.** For a triangle, $2r \le R$.

**Proof.** $0 \le |IO|^2 = R(R - 2r)$ and $R > 0$.

## inradius_le_ninePointCircle_radius

For a triangle, the inradius is at most the radius of the nine-point circle: $r \le R/2$.

**Proof.** Rewrite the nine-point radius as $R/2$ (`ninePointCircle_radius`) and use Euler's inequality via `grw`.
