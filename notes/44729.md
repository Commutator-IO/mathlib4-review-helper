Proves that for squarefree $n$, every residue $a \in \mathbb{Z}/n$ (not only units) satisfies $a^{k\lambda(n)+1} = a$, where $\lambda$ is the Carmichael function (the identity behind RSA correctness). Also proves the converse: if $a^{m+1} = a$ for all $a$ and some $m \ne 0$, then $n$ is squarefree. Together these give an iff, and the PR deduces that a Carmichael number $n$ satisfies $a^n = a$ for every residue. It builds on the existing `carmichael` arithmetic function, `IsCarmichael.squarefree` and `IsCarmichael.carmichael_dvd_sub_one`.

## _root_.Squarefree.zmod_pow_mul_carmichael_add_one

Let $n$ be squarefree. Then for every $k \in \mathbb{N}$ and every $a \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, $a^{k\lambda(n)+1} = a$.

**Proof.** Lift $a$ to its representative $\bar a \in \mathbb{N}$. It suffices to show $n \mid \bar a^{k\lambda(n)+1} - \bar a$. Since $n = \prod_{p \mid n} p$ (squarefree), it is enough to show this for each prime $p \mid n$ (`Finset.prod_primes_dvd`). In $\mathbb{Z}/p$: either $\bar a \equiv 0$, or $p - 1 = \lambda(p) \mid \lambda(n)$ and Fermat's little theorem gives $\bar a^{k\lambda(n)} = 1$.

## squarefree_of_forall_zmod_pow_add_one_eq

Let $m \ne 0$. If $a^{m+1} = a$ for every $a \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, then $n$ is squarefree.

**Proof.** Suppose $p^2 \mid n$ for a prime $p$. Applying the hypothesis to $a = p$ and reducing mod $p^2$ gives $p^{m+1} \equiv p \pmod{p^2}$. But $p^{m+1} \equiv 0 \pmod{p^2}$ since $m+1 \ge 2$, so $p^2 \mid p$, which is impossible.

**Check.** Public theorem with no docstring, and it is not listed in the module docstring.

## squarefree_iff_forall_zmod_pow_carmichael_add_one

For $n \ne 0$: $n$ is squarefree if and only if $a^{\lambda(n)+1} = a$ for every $a \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.

**Proof.** ($\Rightarrow$) is the $k = 1$ case of the first theorem. ($\Leftarrow$) is the previous lemma with $m = \lambda(n)$, which is nonzero because $\lambda(n)$ is the exponent of the finite group $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$.

## IsCarmichael.zmod_pow_eq_self

If $n$ is a Carmichael number, then $a^n = a$ for every $a \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, including residues not coprime to $n$.

**Proof.** $\lambda(n) \mid n - 1$, so $n = d\lambda(n) + 1$ (using $n > 2$). A Carmichael number is squarefree, so the first theorem with $k = d$ applies.
