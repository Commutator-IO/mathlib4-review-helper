Defines `MeromorphicFun`, written $\mathbb{k} \to_{\mathcal M[U]} E$: meromorphic functions $U \to E$ modulo equality off a discrete set (the filter `codiscreteWithin U`). Each class has a canonical representative, the normal form `toMeromorphicNFOn` (used as the `FunLike` coercion), and a well-defined order `orderAt x` at accumulation points of $U$. The quotient is an additive group and $R$-module, a commutative $\mathbb{k}$-algebra when the target is a normed field $\mathbb{k}'$, and a field when $U$ is nontrivial and preconnected (an instance for $U = \mathbb{k}$ in a preconnected space). The derivative is a $\mathbb{k}$-linear map and a `Derivation`, with the product, power, inverse and quotient rules. Supporting lemmas go into `NormalForm.lean` (the value of the normal form at $x$ depends only on the germ on a punctured neighbourhood) and `Order.lean` (a meromorphic function on a preconnected set that is not a.e. zero has an inverse off a discrete set).

Notation for all sections: $\mathbb{k}$ is a nontrivially normed field, $E, E_2, E_3$ are normed $\mathbb{k}$-spaces, $\mathbb{k}'$ is a nontrivially normed field that is a normed $\mathbb{k}$-algebra, $U \subseteq \mathbb{k}$, and $R$ is a normed ring acting boundedly on $E$ and commuting with $\mathbb{k}$. "$\sim$" means equality on $U$ off a discrete set (`=ᶠ[codiscreteWithin U]`). "$x$ is good" means $x \in U$ and $x$ is an accumulation point of $U$.

## MeromorphicFun.setoid

**Definition.** The equivalence relation $f \sim g$ on the subtype of functions meromorphic on $U$.

**Proof.** Reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity come from those of `EventuallyEq`.

## MeromorphicFun

**Definition.** The quotient by `setoid E U`, with notation `𝕜 →ℳ[U] E` and `𝕜 →ℳ E` for $U = \mathbb{k}$.

## apply_eq

**Proof.** Both sides are `toMeromorphicNFAt` at $x$ of two functions that agree on a punctured neighbourhood of $x$ (a codiscrete equality gives this at an accumulation point); conclude with the new `toMeromorphicNFAt_congr`.

## deriv_pow

**Proof.** `simp` with the `Derivation` power rule.

## deriv_inv

$(f^{-1})' = -f'/f^2$, without assuming $U$ connected (so the quotient need not be a field).

**Proof.** Pointwise at points where $f$ is analytic. If $f(x) \ne 0$, the quotient rule. If $f(x) = 0$, both sides are $0$: either $f \equiv 0$ near $x$, or $f$ has positive order at $x$, so $1/f \to \infty$ at $x$, $1/f$ is not differentiable at $x$ and `deriv` returns $0$.

**Proof.** From `deriv_mul` and `deriv_inv`, plus the pointwise identity $g^{-1} = g\,(g^2)^{-1}$ (by `field`).

## orderAt_deriv

## toMeromorphicNFAt_of_meromorphicOrderAt_ne_zero

(In `NormalForm.lean`.) If $\mathrm{ord}_x f \ne 0$, the normal form of $f$ at $x$ takes the value $0$ at $x$ (simp).

**Proof.** Unfold `toMeromorphicNFAt`; if $f$ is not meromorphic at $x$ its order is the junk $0$, which the hypothesis excludes.

## MeromorphicAt.exists_analytic_and_toMeromorphicNFAt_eventuallyEq

If $f$ is meromorphic at $x$ with $\mathrm{ord}_x f = 0$, there is $g$ analytic at $x$ with $g(x) \ne 0$ such that the normal form of $f$ at $x$ equals $g$ on a neighbourhood of $x$.

**Proof.** Take $g$ from `meromorphicOrderAt_eq_int_iff` with $n = 0$. On a punctured neighbourhood the normal form equals $f = g$. At $x$ it equals $g(x)$ by definition of the normal form.

## toMeromorphicNFAt_congr

If $f = g$ on a punctured neighbourhood of $x$, then their normal forms at $x$ take the same value at $x$.

**Proof.** $f$ and $g$ are either both meromorphic at $x$ or both not, with the same order. If the order is $0$, write both normal forms near $x$ as analytic $f', g'$ with $f' = g'$ on a punctured neighbourhood, so $f'(x) = g'(x)$ by the identity principle. Otherwise both values are $0$.

**Check.** The conclusion is only about the value at $x$, not equality of the functions `toMeromorphicNFAt f x` and `toMeromorphicNFAt g x` (which fails away from $x$). A plain `_congr` name suggests the latter.

## mul_inv_eventuallyEq

(`MeromorphicOn` namespace.) If $f : \mathbb{k} \to \mathbb{k}'$ is meromorphic on a preconnected $U$ and not $\sim 0$, then $f \cdot f^{-1} \sim 1$.

**Proof.** Use the criterion `mem_codiscreteWithin_iff_forall_mem_nhdsNE`. At each $x \in U$ the order is not $\top$: otherwise, by the previous lemma, the order would be $\top$ at every point of $U$, which makes $f \sim 0$. Then apply `mul_inv_evnetuallyEq_of_meromorphicOrderAt_ne_top`.

**Check.** The name has no `one`/`_eq_one` part; something like `mul_inv_eventuallyEq_one` would say what $f \cdot f^{-1}$ is equal to.
