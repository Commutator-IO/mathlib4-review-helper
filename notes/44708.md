For an algebra $A$ (not necessarily commutative) over a commutative Artinian ring $R$, shows that an $R$-subalgebra $B$ is closed under inverses of units of $A$ that lie in $B$ and are integral over $R$ (e.g. subalgebras of $\operatorname{End}_K(V)$, $V$ finite-dimensional). Builds on the existing `isUnit_of_isIntegral_of_nonZeroDivisor` (integral non-zero-divisors are units over an Artinian base).

## isUnit_of_isIntegral_of_isUnit

Let $R$ be a commutative Artinian ring, $A$ an $R$-algebra and $B \subseteq A$ an $R$-subalgebra. If $a \in B$ is integral over $R$ and is a unit in $A$, then $a$ is a unit in $B$.

**Proof.** $a$ is integral as an element of $B$ (`isIntegral_algHom_iff` for the injective inclusion) and is a non-zero-divisor in $B$ since it is one in $A$; conclude with `isUnit_of_isIntegral_of_nonZeroDivisor` in $B$.

**Check.** No docstring, and the name `isUnit_of_isIntegral_of_isUnit` reads as circular; something mentioning the subalgebra (e.g. a `Subalgebra.`-namespaced name) may be clearer.

## inv_mem_of_isIntegral

$R$, $A$, $B$ as above. If $u \in A^\times$ lies in $B$ and is integral over $R$, then $u^{-1} \in B$.

**Proof.** By the previous lemma $u$ is the image of a unit $v$ of $B$; since the inclusion $B \to A$ maps $v$ to $u$, $u^{-1}$ is the image of $v^{-1} \in B$.
