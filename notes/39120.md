The PR defines the "norm determinant" $\operatorname{normDet}(f) = \sqrt{\det(f^*f)}$ of a linear map $f : U \to V$ between inner product spaces ($U$ finite-dimensional). It is the factor by which $f$ scales $\dim U$-dimensional volume, and it generalises $|\det f|$ for endomorphisms. The PR then proves the area formula for maps $f : U \to V$ that are injective on a measurable set $s$ and whose derivative is injective at every point of $s$: $\int_s \operatorname{normDet}(f'(x))\,dx = \mathcal H^{\dim U}(f(s))$, where $\mathcal H$ is the Euclidean-normalised Hausdorff measure `μHE`. As an application it computes the length of the unit circle. The proof follows Evans–Gariepy (Lemma 3.3 ff.): cover $s$ by countably many pieces on which $f$ is bi-Lipschitz after a linear change of variables, then let the Lipschitz constant tend to 1. Two lemmas in `MeasureTheory/Function/Jacobian.lean` are generalised from $f : E \to E$ to $f : E \to F$ so they can be used here. `Geometry/Euclidean/Volume/Area.lean` is still a draft: it has helper names like `lemma3_3`, `left` and `glue`, "by aristotle" comments, typos in names, an empty module docstring, and large commented-out blocks containing `sorry`.

## _root_.ContinuousLinearMap.adjoint_toLinearMap

**Proof.** `rfl`.

## normDet

For a linear map $f : U \to V$ over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, with $U$ finite-dimensional: suppose the range $f(U)$ has an orthonormal basis indexed by `Fin (finrank U)`, i.e. $\dim f(U) = \dim U$. Then $\operatorname{normDet} f$ is the norm of the determinant of the matrix of $f : U \to f(U)$, taken in the standard orthonormal basis of $U$ and some chosen such basis of $f(U)$. Otherwise it is $0$.

**Definition.** A real number, defined by cases via `Classical.choice`; it does not depend on the bases chosen (next lemmas).

## normDet_nonneg

$\operatorname{normDet} f \ge 0$.

**Proof.** Unfold the definition; both branches are nonnegative.

## normDet_eq_norm_det_toMatrix_rangeRestrict

For any orthonormal bases $b_U$ of $U$ and $b_V$ of $f(U)$ indexed by the same finite type, $\operatorname{normDet} f = \lVert\det [f]_{b_U, b_V}\rVert$, where $f$ is viewed as a map onto its range.

**Proof.** Change of basis: the two change-of-basis matrices are unitary, so their determinants have norm 1.

## normDet_eq_zero_iff_ker_ne_bot

$\operatorname{normDet} f = 0 \iff \ker f \ne 0$.

**Proof.** If $f$ is injective, $\det(f^*f) \ne 0$ because $\ker(f^*f) = \ker f$. If it is not, $\dim f(U) < \dim U$, so no basis of the required size exists.

## normDet_eq_zero_iff_rank_range_ne

$\operatorname{normDet} f = 0 \iff \dim f(U) \ne \dim U$.

**Proof.** Previous lemma plus rank–nullity.

## normDet_ne_zero_tfae

The following are equivalent: $\operatorname{normDet} f \ne 0$; $\ker f = 0$; $\dim f(U) = \dim U$; $f(U)$ has an orthonormal basis indexed by `Fin (dim U)`; $f$ is injective.

**Proof.** `tfae` from the two previous lemmas.

## orthonormalBasis_range

(private) For injective $f$ and an orthonormal basis $b$ of $U$ indexed by $\iota$, some orthonormal basis of $f(U)$ indexed by the same $\iota$.

**Definition.** The chosen basis from the TFAE, reindexed along a cardinality equivalence.

## normDet_eq_zero_tfae

The following are equivalent: $\operatorname{normDet} f = 0$; $\ker f \ne 0$; $\dim f(U) \ne \dim U$; $\dim f(U) < \dim U$; no orthonormal basis of $f(U)$ is indexed by `Fin (dim U)`; $f$ is not injective.

**Proof.** `tfae` from the previous lemmas and $\dim f(U) \le \dim U$.

## normDet_eq_norm_det_toMatrix

If $b_U$ and $b_V$ are orthonormal bases of $U$ and of the whole of $V$, indexed by the same finite type (so $\dim U = \dim V$), then $\operatorname{normDet} f = \lVert\det [f]_{b_U,b_V}\rVert$.

**Proof.** If $f$ is surjective, transport $b_V$ to the range and use the range version. Otherwise both sides are $0$: a nonzero determinant would make $f$ an equivalence.

## normDet_eq_norm_det

For an endomorphism $f : U \to U$, $\operatorname{normDet} f = \lVert\det f\rVert$.

**Proof.** Previous lemma with the standard orthonormal basis on both sides.

## _root_.LinearIsometry.normDet_eq_one

A linear isometry $f : U \to V$ has $\operatorname{normDet} f = 1$.

**Proof.** Its matrix onto its range in orthonormal bases is unitary.

## normDet_id

$\operatorname{normDet}(\mathrm{id}_U) = 1$.

**Proof.** Special case of the isometry lemma.

## normDet_subtype

The inclusion of a subspace $p \hookrightarrow U$ has norm determinant $1$.

**Proof.** It is an isometry.

## normDet_of_subsingleton

If $U = 0$, then $\operatorname{normDet} f = 1$ for every $f$.

**Proof.** The determinant of the empty matrix is $1$.

## normDet_zero

$\operatorname{normDet}(0) = 0^{\dim U}$, i.e. $0$ unless $U = 0$.

**Proof.** Through `nontriviality`, the zero map has nonzero kernel.

## normDet_smul

$\operatorname{normDet}(c f) = \lvert c\rvert^{\dim U} \operatorname{normDet} f$.

**Proof.** For $c \ne 0$ and $f$ injective, the ranges agree and `det_smul` applies. Otherwise both sides vanish.

## normDet_neg

$\operatorname{normDet}(-f) = \operatorname{normDet} f$.

**Proof.** `normDet_smul` with $c = -1$.

## _root_.ContinuousLinearMap.normDet_sq

For a continuous linear map $f : U \to V$ with $V$ complete (possibly infinite-dimensional), $(\operatorname{normDet} f)^2 = \det(f^* f)$, as an equation in the scalar field $\mathbb{K}$.

**Proof.** If $f$ is injective, write $f = \iota \circ f|^{f(U)}$ with $\iota^*\iota = 1$. Then $\det(f^*f) = \overline{\det M}\det M$ for the matrix $M$ of $f$ onto its range. Otherwise both sides are $0$ because $\ker(f^*f) = \ker f$.

## normDet_sq

The same identity $(\operatorname{normDet} f)^2 = \det(f^* f)$ for a linear map $f : U \to V$ with $V$ finite-dimensional.

**Proof.** Apply the continuous version to `f.toContinuousLinearMap`.

## normDet_sq_eq_det_gram

For any orthonormal basis $(b_i)$ of $U$, $(\operatorname{normDet} f)^2 = \det\big(\langle f b_i, f b_j\rangle\big)_{i,j}$, the Gram determinant of the images.

**Proof.** If $f$ is injective, the Gram matrix is $M^H M$ for the matrix $M$ of $f$ onto its range. Otherwise the vectors $f b_i$ are linearly dependent, so the Gram determinant is $0$.

## normDet_comp

For $f : U \to V$ and $g : V \to W$: $\operatorname{normDet}(g \circ f) = \operatorname{normDet}(g|_{f(U)}) \cdot \operatorname{normDet} f$.

**Proof.** If $f$ and $g \circ f$ are injective, multiply the matrices in bases $b_U$, $b_{f(U)}$, $b_{g f(U)}$. The degenerate cases give $0 = 0$.

## normDet_comp_of_finrank_eq

If $V$ is finite-dimensional and $\dim U = \dim V$, then $\operatorname{normDet}(g\circ f) = \operatorname{normDet} g \cdot \operatorname{normDet} f$.

**Proof.** If $f$ is onto, use `normDet_comp` with $f(U) = V$. Otherwise $f$ is not injective and both sides vanish.

## normDet_codRestrict

Restricting the codomain of $f$ to a subspace $p \supseteq f(U)$ does not change $\operatorname{normDet} f$.

**Proof.** $f = p.\mathrm{subtype} \circ f|^p$, then `normDet_comp`; the restricted inclusion is an isometry.

## normDet_eq_prod_singularValues

If $V$ is finite-dimensional, $\operatorname{normDet} f = \prod_{i < \dim U} \sigma_i(f)$, the product of the first $\dim U$ singular values.

**Proof.** Square both sides, use `normDet_sq`, $\sigma_i^2 = $ eigenvalues of $f^*f$, and determinant = product of eigenvalues for a symmetric map.

## normDet_eq_abs_det

For a real endomorphism, $\operatorname{normDet} f = |\det f|$.

**Proof.** `normDet_eq_norm_det`.

## hausdorffMeasure_image

For a real linear map $f : U \to V$ (with $V$ any real inner product space) and any set $s \subseteq U$, with $n = \dim U$: $\mathcal{H}^n(f(s)) = \operatorname{normDet}(f)\, \mathcal{H}^n(s)$ (unnormalised Hausdorff measure `μH`).

**Proof.** If $f$ is injective, factor $f$ as an isometry $U \cong f(U) \hookrightarrow V$ after an endomorphism of $U$. The isometry preserves $\mathcal H^n$, and Haar measure scales by $|\det|$ under the endomorphism. If $f$ is not injective, $f(s)$ lies in a subspace of dimension $< n$, which has $\mathcal H^n$-measure $0$.

## euclideanHausdorffMeasure_image

The same scaling for the Euclidean-normalised Hausdorff measure: $\mu_{HE}^n(f(s)) = \operatorname{normDet}(f)\,\mu_{HE}^n(s)$, $n = \dim U$.

**Proof.** `μHE` is a constant multiple of `μH`; rewrite and commute the scalars.

## euclideanHausdorffMeasure_image_eq_normDet_mul_volume

$\mu_{HE}^{\dim U}(f(s)) = \operatorname{normDet}(f)\cdot \mathrm{vol}(s)$, with $\mathrm{vol}$ the Lebesgue measure on $U$.

**Proof.** Previous lemma and `euclideanHausdorffMeasure_eq_volume`.
