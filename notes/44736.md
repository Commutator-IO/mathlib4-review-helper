Characterizes, over a ring $R$ of prime characteristic $p$, the polynomials all of whose Hasse derivatives $D^{(k)}$ of orders $0 < k < p^e$ vanish: exactly those whose exponents are all divisible by $p^e$, i.e. the polynomials in $X^{p^e}$ (images of `expand R (p ^ e)`). The arithmetic input is a new Lucas-type lemma: $p^e \mid n$ iff $p \mid \binom{n}{k}$ for all $0 < k < p^e$. Also adds a coefficientwise vanishing criterion for `hasseDeriv` and the Lucas reference to `references.bib`.

## forall_hasseDeriv_eq_zero_iff

For a ring $R$ of characteristic $p$ ($p$ prime), $e \in \mathbb{N}$ and $f \in R[X]$: $D^{(k)} f = 0$ for all $0 < k < p^e$ if and only if $p^e \mid n$ for every $n$ with $\mathrm{coeff}_n f \ne 0$.

**Proof.** By `hasseDeriv_eq_zero_iff`, $D^{(k)} f = 0$ iff $\binom{n}{k} a_n = 0$ for all $n$; in characteristic $p$, $c \cdot a = 0$ iff $p \mid c$ or $a = 0$ (a natural number not divisible by $p$ is a unit in $R$). Then swap quantifiers and apply `Choose.pow_dvd_iff_forall_dvd_choose`.

## forall_hasseDeriv_eq_zero_iff_exists_expand

For a commutative ring $R$ of characteristic $p$ and $f \in R[X]$: $D^{(k)} f = 0$ for all $0 < k < p^e$ if and only if $f = g(X^{p^e})$ for some $g \in R[X]$ (i.e. $f = $ `expand R (p ^ e) g`).

**Proof.** Via the previous theorem: if all exponents are divisible by $p^e$, take $g = $ `contract (p ^ e) f` and compare coefficients (`coeff_expand`, `coeff_contract`); conversely the coefficients of `expand` vanish off multiples of $p^e$.

## hasseDeriv_eq_zero_iff

For a semiring $R$, $k \in \mathbb{N}$ and $f = \sum a_n X^n \in R[X]$: $D^{(k)} f = 0$ iff $\binom{n}{k} a_n = 0$ in $R$ for every $n$.

**Proof.** The coefficient of $X^m$ in $D^{(k)} f$ is $\binom{m+k}{k} a_{m+k}$ (`hasseDeriv_coeff`); for $n < k$ the binomial vanishes, for $n \ge k$ read off the coefficient at $n - k$.

## pow_dvd_iff_forall_dvd_choose

(In namespace `Choose`.) For a prime $p$ and $n, e \in \mathbb{N}$: $p^e \mid n$ if and only if $p \mid \binom{n}{k}$ for every $0 < k < p^e$.

**Proof.** ($\Rightarrow$) Induction on $e$ using the Lucas congruence $\binom{n}{k} \equiv \binom{n \bmod p}{k \bmod p}\binom{\lfloor n/p\rfloor}{\lfloor k/p\rfloor} \pmod p$: since $p \mid n$, the first factor is $\binom{0}{k \bmod p}$, zero unless $p \mid k$, and if $p \mid k$ reduce to $n/p$, $k/p$, $e-1$. ($\Leftarrow$) If $p^e \nmid n$, write $n = p^v m$ with $p \nmid m$, $v < e$; Lucas gives $\binom{p^v m}{p^v} \equiv \binom{m}{1} = m \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$, contradicting the hypothesis at $k = p^v$.
