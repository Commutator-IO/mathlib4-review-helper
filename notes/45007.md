Two extensions of the Leibniz rule for a derivation $D : A \to M$ (from a commutative $R$-algebra $A$ to an $A$-module $M$): the product rule for a finite product, and the power rule for integer powers of a unit. The latter is the commutative-ring analogue of the existing field lemma `Derivation.leibniz_zpow`, generalising `leibniz_pow` and `leibniz_of_mul_eq_one`.

## leibniz_prod

For commutative semirings $R$, $A$ with $A$ an $R$-algebra, an $A$-module $M$, an $R$-derivation $D : A \to M$, a finite set $s$ and $f : s \to A$,
$$D\Big(\prod_{i\in s} f(i)\Big) = \sum_{i\in s}\Big(\prod_{j\in s,\ j\ne i} f(j)\Big)\cdot D(f(i)).$$

**Proof.** Induction on $s$ (`Finset.induction_on`): for $s \cup \{a\}$, apply the binary Leibniz rule and the induction hypothesis, then match the sums termwise using `erase_insert` and `prod_insert`.

## leibniz_zpow_units

For a commutative ring $A$ (an $R$-algebra), an $A$-module $M$ that is an additive group, an $R$-derivation $D : A \to M$, a unit $u \in A^\times$ and $n \in \mathbb{Z}$,
$$D(u^n) = n \cdot u^{n-1} \cdot D(u),$$
where $u^n, u^{n-1}$ are computed in $A^\times$ and the factor $n$ acts by $\mathbb{Z}$-scalar multiplication.

**Proof.** Case split on $n$: for $n = k \ge 0$ reduce to `leibniz_pow` (with a further case $k = 0$ / $k+1$); for $n = -(k+1)$ write $u^n = (u^{-1})^{k+1}$, apply `leibniz_pow` and `leibniz_of_mul_eq_one` for $u^{-1}$, convert $\mathbb{N}$/$\mathbb{Z}$-smul to scalar multiplication in $A$, and close with `push_cast; ring`.
